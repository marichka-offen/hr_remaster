The British Military Presence in America

1660 — 1720

Regular regiments, independent companies, and militia forces across the North American colonies and the Caribbean — a comprehensive survey of Britain's early military footprint.

Background

The presence of British regular army forces in the American colonies in the late 17th century and early 18th century was sporadic at best. This period in which Britain was becoming an empire at war intermittently with other European Powers, was a time when defending colonial possessions was becoming, but was not yet, a major priority.

Regular Regiments

Regular regiments, in whole or in part, were involved in both garrison and expeditionary missions in North America and the Caribbean. How the regiments were equipped was a function of how much the regimental commander was willing to spend on his soldiers and where the British army was generally in its transition away from pike to bayonet, from matchlock to firelock, bandoleer of charges to cartridge boxes.

Uniform Colors Key

Throughout this article, regiment colors are noted as (coat/facing) — for example, (red/yellow) means a red coat with yellow facings. These colors are shown as visual swatches next to unit names in the timeline and garrison sections below.

Independent Companies

The Independent Companies were composed of officers and soldiers sent from Britain and not raised locally. Unlike other units, they wore Royal Livery colors — red coats faced blue. Commanders were responsible for clothing, arming and equipping their soldiers. Once a soldier came over with an Independent Company they rarely went back to Britain.

An Independent Company had 50–100 soldiers with one Captain, one to two Lieutenants, three sergeants, three corporals and 2 drummers. These were not prestigious postings for ambitious officers.

Militia

The militia comprised all able-bodied male civilians usually between ages 18 and 60, led by local prominent community leaders. They either brought their own arms or were armed by their commanders. Militia rotated through forts, patrolled frontier areas and coastal regions, and were involved in expeditionary operations.

The Role of Women

Women had a critical role in the armies of the late 17th and early 18th centuries, to the point that armies would feed 15 women per company to ensure the execution of essential logistical roles of cooking, medical support, and repairing and washing clothes. Wherever British soldiers went, their women went too.
