The Eighty Years War

1568 — 1648

The Dutch revolt against Hapsburg Spain — a conflict spanning eight decades that birthed a nation, transformed European warfare, and reshaped the balance of power across the continent.

General Event

As a result of wars, marriages and political maneuvering, most of the region currently known as Belgium and The Netherlands were in the hands of the Dukes of Burgundy. Charles V, the Holy Roman Emperor, was the ruler of this region by 1515. By the mid 16th century the Hapsburg-controlled Netherlands was comprised of 3 million people with 300 cities in 17 provinces — the southern three Walloon speaking and the northern 14 speaking Dutch.

Antwerp was one of the economic centers of Europe and the place from which gold bullion from the New World was exchanged. The Hapsburg-Valois Wars with France ended in 1559, making the movement of people and ideas easier in Western Europe. One of the ideas on the move was Calvinist Protestantism out of Geneva — and it found particular support among the numerous lower class, lesser noble and townsfolk in the Netherlands.

1555–67
Gathering Storm · 18 events
Political
October 1555
Charles V Abdicates

Emperor Charles V, a Burgundian who spoke Flemish, abdicates and the Hapsburg Empire is divided — Spain and the Netherlands pass to his son Phillip II.

"The Spaniards seem wise, and are madmen; the French seem madmen, and are wise."

— Charles V
1555

Phillip II appoints William of Orange as provincial governor (Stadthouder) of Holland, Utrecht and Zeeland. The Count of Egmont was appointed Stadthouder of Flanders and Artois.

Battle
10 August 1557
Battle of Saint Quentin

French relief column routed by cavalry force under command of Count Egmont during the joint Hapsburg-English siege. The city was sacked on 28 August by Hapsburg-English forces.

1558

William of Orange persuades the States-General to grant Phillip II a 9-year subsidy in exchange for the granting of liberties and removal of 3,000 Spanish mercenaries.

Treaty
June 1559
Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis

Peace between France and Hapsburgs ends the Italian Wars and opens Western Europe to the free movement of ideas — including Calvinist Protestantism.

7 August 1559

Phillip II's last visit to the Netherlands. Margaret of Parma appointed Regent. Antoine Granvelle appointed President of the Council of State.

1559–1565

Steady increase of Calvinist influence from the German States and France into the Netherlands. Spanish troops depart in 1561. Granvelle appointed Primate of the Church of the Netherlands in 1562.

Political
November 1565
Council of State Resignations

William of Orange, Count of Horn, and Lamoral of Egmont resign from the Council of State over persecution of Protestants and loss of influence over selection of Bishops.

The Year of Miracles
Spring 1566 – Spring 1567
Annus Mirabilis

Louis of Nassau and Count Brederode present the Compromise of Breda to Margaret of Parma requesting suspension of the Edict of Blood and Inquisition. The Iconoclast Fury (beeldenstorm) results in the defacing of Catholic Churches throughout the Netherlands.

Battle
13 March 1567
Battle of Oosterweel

Calvinist rebel "Beggar" army (geuzen leger) defeated — the first military engagement of the revolt.

Political
22 August 1567
Duke of Alba Arrives

Duke of Alba arrives in Brussels with an army of 9,000 to quell unrest. He establishes the "Council of Troubles" (Council of Blood) — by 1573, 12,000 cases tried, 1,000 executions, 9,000 confiscations.

9 September 1567

Arrest of Egmont, general and former companion to Alba and Phillip II, along with his fellow Protestant sympathizer Horn. Approximately 60,000 flee the Netherlands for neighboring German states and England.

1568–73
Open Revolt · 22 events
Battle
23 May 1568
Battle of Heiligerlee

German rebel force of 2,500 horse and 7,000 foot under Louis of Nassau defeats the army of the Count of Aremberg near Groningen — the first rebel military victory.

"Nunc aut nunquam, Recuperare aut mori" — Freedom for the Fatherland and conscience.

— Inscription on Louis of Nassau's colors
5 June 1568
Egmont and Horn Executed

Egmont and Horn are executed for high treason in Brussels, turning them into martyrs for the revolt.

Battle
21 July 1568
Battle of Jemmingen

Rebel and Huguenot force under Prince Louis of Nassau routed along the river Ems. Count Adolf of Nassau killed. A devastating reversal after Heiligerlee.

February 1569

William of Orange gives Sea Beggars their first Letters of Commission. They are based in Emden, La Rochelle (a Huguenot-controlled port in France), and Dover in England.

Political
March 1569
The Tenth Penny Tax

Duke of Alba forces the States-General to enact a ruinous 10% sales tax and 5% property tax to fund an army he — not the States-General — would control.

"Long live the Beggars! Christians, ye must cry. Long live the Beggars! pluck up courage then. Long live the Beggars! if ye would not die."

— Beggar's Song, 1570
Turning Point
1 April 1572
Capture of Brill (Den Briel)

The Spanish garrison, called away to reinforce the French border, leaves Brill undefended. Count Lumley de la Marck occupies the town with 600 Walloon, Dutch, Scots and English Sea Beggars. This single event triggers a cascade of towns declaring for the revolt.

April–July 1572

Veere, Vlissingen, Enkhuizen, Hoorn, Alkmaar, Haarlem and most of the towns of Walcheren Island join the revolt. All proclaim William of Orange Stadhouder.

Major Siege
Siege of Haarlem
7 months
4 December 1572 – 12 July 1573

City defended by a garrison of 3,000 Scots, French, Germans and Walloons, 600 armed citizens and a regiment of 300 women commanded by Vrau Kenau Hasselaer.

  • DecRelief column under De La Marck routed. De La Marck captured and executed.
  • Dec–JanResupply by French, Scots, English and Walloon forces across frozen lake from Sassenhiem.
  • FebSpanish breach outer wall only to find an inner wall behind it.
  • Mar–AprGarrison conducts repeated sallies against besiegers.
  • AprilFinal attempt by William of Orange to relieve garrison fails.
  • 12 JulyCity surrenders, ransomed for 24,000 guilders. Garrison of 1,800 English, Scots, and Huguenots killed.

"They earn their meals through the mouth of a cannon."

— William of Orange, 26 February 1573, describing the Sea Beggars
Major Siege
Siege of Alkmaar
7 weeks
21 August – 8 October 1573

Spanish forced to raise the siege after the Dutch cut the dykes, flooding the surrounding countryside. This marks the first time the rebels successfully use water as a weapon.

"Bij Alkmaar begint de victorie" — At Alkmaar began the victory.

— Dutch saying
Naval Battle
10 October 1573
Battle of Zuider Zee

Sea Beggar victory under Cornelis Dirckszoon. Most of the Spanish fleet, including the flagship "Inquisition," run aground. The battle leaves the Scheldt Estuary in Sea Beggar hands.

1574–79
Independence Takes Shape · 16 events
Battle
14 April 1574
Battle of Mook

Spanish victory over a rebel relief force for the siege of Leiden. Louis of Nassau, Henry of Nassau, and Duke Christopher killed — a devastating loss for the House of Orange.

Defining Siege
Siege of Leiden
5 months
26 May – October 1574

Garrison of five companies of Burgher Guard holds the city against overwhelming odds.

  • JulyDykes along the Meuse and Yssel rivers broken to prevent infantry attack on city walls.
  • 3 OctoberWater levels rise permitting Sea Beggar ships to approach. Spanish forces flee as the city is relieved.

"Here is my sword; plunge it, if you will, into my heart, and divide my flesh among you to appease your hunger; but expect no surrender as long as I am alive."

— Burgomeister of Leiden, 1574
February 1575

Leiden University founded out of gratitude to the city and to meet the increasing need for Calvinist pastors — one of the most prestigious universities in Europe to this day.

Massacre
4 November 1576
The Spanish Fury

Spanish mutineers seize and sack Antwerp in what becomes known as the Spaanse Furie — one of the most devastating sackings in European history.

Treaty
8 November 1576
Pacification of Ghent

As a direct result of the Spanish Fury, the States-General agrees to the Pacificatie van Gent — calling for removal of all Spanish soldiers and unity to expel all foreign forces from the Netherlands.

Battle
31 January 1578
Battle of Gembloux

Rebel army defeated by the Army of Don Juan of Austria. The Scots Brigade is decimated. A major setback that shows the military weakness of the rebel coalition.

Founding Moment
January 1579
Union of Utrecht

Seven northern provinces — Holland, Zeeland, Utrecht, Friesland, Groningen, Overijsel, and Gelderland — sign the Union of Utrecht, forming the Republic of the United Provinces with William of Orange as leader. The southern provinces sign the Union of Arras and accept Spanish authority.

"Persuade yourself that 500 of either English, Scottish, Burgundian, Walloons, French, Italians, Albanese, or Spanish is worth 1,500 Almains."

— On German soldiers, Sir Roger Williams, 1618
Major Siege
Siege of Maastricht
4 months
February – 29 June 1579

Townspeople as well as the States army garrison involved in defense against Spanish forces. The fall of Maastricht demonstrates the continued power of the Spanish Army.

1580–88
Survival & Sovereignty · 14 events
August 1580

Prince of Orange declared an outlaw by Phillip II, placing a bounty on his head and making him a target for assassination.

Declaration
26 July 1581
Act of Abjuration (Plakkaat van Verlatinghe)

The States-General declares the rule of Phillip II no longer valid — often called the "Dutch Declaration of Independence." It argued that a monarch who tyrannizes his subjects forfeits his right to rule.

"When he does not do so... should be taken not as a Monarch, but as a Tyrant."

— The Act of Abjuration, 1581
17 January 1583
French Seizure of Antwerp Fails

Duke of Anjou's attempt to seize Antwerp with French soldiers defeated by armed citizens. Attempts on Bruges and Ostend also fail — though Aalst, Vilvoorde and Dunkirk fall.

1583–1584

Spanish forces capture Zutphen, Dunkirk, Nieuport, Ypres, Bruges and numerous other cities. The military tide turns sharply against the rebels as the Duke of Parma's campaign gains momentum.

Assassination
10 July 1584
William of Orange Assassinated

William of Orange, the father of the revolt, is assassinated at Delft — the first head of state to be killed by a handgun. His son Maurice of Nassau succeeds him and will prove to be one of history's great military reformers.
